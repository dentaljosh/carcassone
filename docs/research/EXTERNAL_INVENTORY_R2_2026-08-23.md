## 0. Verdict up front

**NOT "nothing stronger found" — but only one candidate, and its strength claim is transitive, not measured against us.**

Exactly **one** program in the entire sweep carries a *published, measured* head-to-head win over a JCloisterZone-AI-derived opponent, in our exact rules scope (2p, base game, farmers, modern 3-pts-per-city field scoring), under an open licence, with a working headless path:

> **Carcasum** (Yannick Müller, master's thesis, 2014, C++/Qt, AGPL-3.0, `TripleWhy/Carcasum`) — its `MCTSPlayer` scores **84.0 % against its own port of the JCloisterZone AI** (n=208, 5 s/move, ±4.19 % at 90 %), and its flat `MonteCarloPlayer` scores **74 %**.

Naive transitive arithmetic puts Carcasum's MCTS at **≈ +288 elo over "a JCZ AI"**, i.e. **≈ +188 elo above our champion's +100.4**. Treat that number as a **hypothesis to test, not a measurement** — §3.1 lists five reasons it can be wrong, at least two of which are known to bite. But it is the first externally-published claim we have found that *could* place a runnable program above the champion, and Carcasum is genuinely harnessable.

Everything else found in round 2 is **at or below JCZ tier**, unmatchable, or out of scope. R1's headline ("no candidate has a verified strength claim above JCZ") was correct *for the candidates R1 examined* — it missed Carcasum's thesis, which R1 listed as a repo with "no headless mode documented" and never opened.

If Carcasum fails on inspection or in a pilot match, the machine-reference route **is** exhausted and the human bridge (§5) becomes the only path.

## 2. Ranked shortlist

### #1 — Carcasum (`TripleWhy/Carcasum`) · AGPL-3.0 · **recommend: pilot it**

The only candidate that clears every gate at once. Scope is exactly ours (2 players, base game only, farmers with the modern per-field 3-pts-per-completed-city rule, chance nodes for the tile draw rather than a determinized peek). Matchability is genuinely good and better than R1 believed: there is a headless `tournament` binary, `Game::setNextTileProvider` with a `HistoryProvider` lets us **force our exact deck order** (so deck-paired CRN works the same way `ForcedDrawTilePack` does for JCZ), `storeToFile`/`loadFromFile` of the move history gives us a replay oracle for the divergence audit, and `simStep(MoveHistoryEntry)` lets an external driver push moves in. It vendors **JCloisterZone's own `basic.xml` tile definitions**, which means the tile-identity work we already did for JCZ (`tests/data/jcz/TILE_MAPPING.tsv`, 32/32 kinds, 72/72 tiles) should transfer with a diff of the 2014 vs 2023 XML rather than a fresh mapping. And — uniquely among everything found — **it has a budget knob**: playouts or seconds per move, which is exactly the property JCZ lacks and the property a non-saturating ruler needs. Its own doubling ladder (Table 5.5) prices that knob at roughly **+44 elo per doubling and shrinking** at the top of the measured range (16384 vs 8192 playouts → 56.33 %), so the knob is real but not free money.

The published claim is that `MCTSPlayer` at 5 s/move (≈42,879 playouts/turn on a 2014 Xeon E3-1230v2) scores **84.0 %** against Carcasum's port of the JCloisterZone AI (n=208, ±4.19 % at 90 % confidence; "scoring rate" = (wins + 0.5·draws)/n, i.e. directly elo-convertible). Note the configuration tested was *not even their best*: it used Score-Portion reward + random playouts, while the thesis concludes a normalised Heyden evaluation is the better reward and ε-greedy the better playout policy. **Do not treat +188-elo-above-champion as a finding** — treat it as the reason to spend a day on the harness.

One more reason this is worth doing beyond the ruler: Carcasum sits at a point in the design space our program **abandoned early** — a very large budget of full random rollouts, versus our small budget of heuristic-leaf simulations (~42.8k random playouts/turn vs the champion's k8×1376). A match is simultaneously an out-of-lineage rating *and* an independent test of that abandonment.

### #2 — Carcassonne-SGE (Innsbruck, 2026) · licence unstated · **recommend: watch, don't build**

Architecturally the most interesting agent found, and the freshest (June 2026): a PUCT-MCTS whose prior and rollout policy come from a heuristic **tuned by a genetic algorithm** (`VerificationHelper/HeuristicOptimization`), plus an ensemble variant that samples multiple tile orders — which their own `ParallelArena` found to be the best of their agents. The environment is serious engineering (union-find areas, annotation-generated bitpacking, deepcopy optimised for MCTS) and farmers are implemented correctly (`ScoringManager.collectFieldPoints`, 3 points per completed connected city). Two things stop it: it is bound to the SGE framework with no external protocol, and — decisively — **the agents optimise the *cooperative* objective**, two players maximising a combined score. Re-targeting them to competitive margin is a behavioural rewrite, not a config change, and until that is done there is no reason to think they are strong at *our* game. There is also no licence file, which matters if anything is vendored. Revisit if they publish a competitive arena result, or if Carcasum falls through and we want a second shot.

### #3 — a *constructed* stronger reference: wrap JCZ's own `LegacyRanking` in a search · **recommend: hold as the fallback**

Not something found in the wild, but the cheapest way to a stronger-than-JCZ opponent, and it belongs on this list because it is what Carcasum effectively demonstrates in reverse (their MCTS beat their 1-ply JCZ port by 84 %). We already hold a **byte-identical port of JCZ's `LegacyRanking`** in `scripts/jcz_match/java/com/jcloisterzone/ai/`, with the port comment attesting that every numeric constant and formula matches the 4.x original. Putting an n-ply or MCTS search on top of that evaluation would produce an opponent with a *tunable* budget and a *foreign* evaluation function — plugging JCZ's one structural weakness (1-ply, no opponent reply). **Honest caveat that must be stated whenever it is used:** such an opponent is only *half* out-of-lineage. The eval is theirs; the search would be ours, and would inherit our search's blind spots. It is a better *ruler* than JCZ (non-saturating) but a *weaker* independence claim than JCZ (shared search DNA), and the two properties trade off. Do not do this before Carcasum is tried, because Carcasum is fully out-of-lineage on both halves.

### Not shortlisted, but do these cheaply if convenient

- **`fancarpedia/JCloisterZoneEngine`** and **`YetAnotherSpieskowcy/Carcassonne-Engine`** are two independent, actively-maintained, golden-test-validated Carcassonne rule engines (TypeScript and Go). Neither has an AI, so neither is a ruler — but each is a **free third referee** for the `fixed_v1` rules profile. The JCZ oracle currently rests on **one** reference implementation and the VALIDATION_REPORT names that as residual risk #5. A second referee retires that risk for the cost of a replay adapter.

## 3. Honesty section

### 3.1 Why the 84 % might not transport — five failure modes, ranked

1. **Carcasum's "JCZ Player" is not JCZ.** The thesis states plainly that the adaptation "does not play exactly like the original JCloisterZone", that a bug in JCZ's undo management was found and *omitted* from the port, and that the port is "much too slow". It is a 2014 adaptation of the **JCZ 1.x/2.x-era** AI; our `LegacyAiPlayer` is the **4.x** class. Two different ports of two different vintages. **This is the single biggest reason the transitive number can be wrong.**
2. **A corroborating red flag in their own data.** In Carcasum's ladder, `JCZPlayer` beats their hand-written greedy `SimplePlayer v3` by only **51.28 %** — i.e. their JCZ port is statistically *level with a simple greedy heuristic*. If our JCZ reference is meaningfully stronger than a simple greedy heuristic (which our own leaf-vs-search history suggests), then their JCZ port is **weaker than ours**, and the 84 % overstates Carcasum's standing against our reference. Alternatively their SimplePlayer v3 is unusually good. Either way this must be resolved by a direct match, not by arithmetic.
3. **Elo is not transitive here, and this project has already been burned by exactly that.** The leaf effect is documented non-transitive (`clean_eval/CLEAN_EVAL_AUDIT.md`), and CL-070 showed a same-family anchor mis-ordering a +50 elo contrast *including the sign*. Chaining two win-rates through two different builds of a third program is a weaker inference than either of those.
4. **A real rules divergence exists and must be patched.** Thesis §2.3, "Rules Used": Carcasum deliberately keeps the **original-2000 tiny-city exception** — a completed 2-tile city scores **2 points, not 4**. That is a material scoring difference from C2/C3 and from `fixed_v1`. It is small and localised (`CityNode` scoring) and looks one-line-patchable, but it is a live divergence and it is exactly the class of thing the R9 audit exists to catch. Field scoring itself is fine: they explicitly use the *current* per-field, 3-points-per-completed-city rule, which matches us.
5. **Hardware and budget are not matched.** 42,879 playouts/turn was 5 s on a 2014 Xeon E3-1230v2. On a 5900XT the same 5 s buys far more, so a modern Carcasum is stronger than the thesis's Carcasum — which cuts *toward* the candidate, but means the thesis's numbers are not the numbers we would measure. Conversely our champion is ~1.3–4.8 s/move, so a 5 s/move Carcasum is a roughly fair wall-clock pairing, and wall-clock parity (not playout parity) is the right axis for a strength claim.

### 3.2 MEASURED vs VIBES, stated plainly

- **MEASURED (published, with n and confidence bounds):** Carcasum MCTS 84.0 % vs Carcasum's JCZ port (n=208, ±4.19 % @90 %); Carcasum flat MC 74 % vs same; Carcasum's doubling ladder (n=2000 per rung). All from the thesis, read directly from the PDF, not from a summary.
- **MEASURED (ours):** champion vs real JCZ `LegacyAiPlayer`, wr 0.6406 / +100.4 ± 12.3 elo (n=800, deck-paired, `fixed_v1`+R9).
- **INFERRED:** everything about how those two connect. Carcasum-vs-champion is **unmeasured**.
- **VIBES:** the Asmodee "Conqueror" tier being weak (forum/review reports); `mmbednarek`'s MCTS claim; the strength of every unlicensed coursework repo in §1a's tail.
- **UNKNOWN and stated as such:** Carcassonne-SGE's competitive strength — it has never been measured against anything outside its own course.

### 3.3 Rules-divergence risk register for the #1 candidate

Before any Carcasum match, the same audit discipline as the JCZ oracle applies. Known items now: **(a)** the tiny-city 2-vs-4 rule (§3.1 item 4) — confirmed divergent, must be patched or the match is void; **(b)** tile-definition drift — Carcasum vendors JCZ's `basic.xml` circa 2014 vs the `basic:2` set we validated at JCZ rev `29a1561` (2023), so diff the XML including the R9-relevant half-edge declarations before trusting the mapping; **(c)** board bounds — our 35×35 grid / 25×25 action window caps are ours, Carcasum's are unknown and need checking (the JCZ audit's residual risk #1/#2 recurs verbatim); **(d)** tie-break and majority-ownership semantics — inferred from score agreement only, never diffed at feature level, same blind spot as the JCZ oracle; **(e)** unplaceable-tile handling — the thesis notes games are 71 turns "except when a tile is drawn that has no valid placements", so a redraw-vs-discard convention exists and must be compared against `fixed_v1`'s redraw fix.

## 5. Human-bridge intel — Board Game Arena ToS posture

**Read this as pricing an option, not proposing one. Nothing here recommends deploying a bot against BGA's terms, and nothing was attempted.**

**What the ToS and docs actually permit:**

- **Automated play is prohibited.** BGA's Terms of Service prohibit cheating and the use of automated scripts, and specifically prohibit anything that makes moves without human input. Account suspension is the stated remedy. There is no bot-account programme, no research exemption, and no precedent we could find of one being granted.
- **There is no API.** BGA's own developer wiki states flatly: *"There is no framework support (now) for AI/Bots, so it is all custom stuff."* The bots that do exist (Conspiracy, Glow, Crew, Crew Deepsea, Tapestry) are BGA-internal implementations of published solo "Automa" rules, not AI, and none is Carcassonne. Can't Stop is the sole game with a real AI opponent and BGA staff decline to describe how it works. **Carcassonne on BGA has no AI opponent at all** — re-confirming R1.
- **Scraping is separately prohibited** and replays are login-gated with undisclosed rate limits and no export API ([PRO_GAME_RECORDS_INVENTORY_2026-08-14.md](PRO_GAME_RECORDS_INVENTORY_2026-08-14.md)).

**So the posture is:** BGA is closed for *live automated play* (ToS), closed for *bulk record extraction* (ToS + no API), and open only for **manual human play** and **whatever a written permission grant covers**. This matches the standing repo position — `measurement/TOURNAMENT_LANDSCAPE_MEMO_20260728.md`: *"Board Game Arena is a closed door. Its ToS forbids both engine assistance AND protocol-level automation; there is no API, no bot-account pathway, no research precedent."*

**What that costs us, priced:** the human bridge via BGA cannot be an *automated ladder climb*. If the machine-reference route exhausts, the realistic BGA-shaped options are (a) the **records-mining** route already in flight — Joshua's permission request sent 2026-08-14 via BGA support ([BGA_PERMISSION_EMAIL_DRAFT.md](BGA_PERMISSION_EMAIL_DRAFT.md), ~100–500 identified WTCOC/top-ELO 2p base-game replays, non-commercial, explicit no-automation commitment) — which yields *strong-human game records*, not a live rating; or (b) **a human operator relaying the engine's moves in a sanctioned exhibition**, which is a negotiated arrangement with organisers, not a technical one, and which is what the tournament-landscape memo was scoping. Neither is blocked by anything we control, and neither is fast. The value of §2's Carcasum pilot is precisely that it is fast by comparison.

**One relevant non-BGA datum:** the **SYNCS Bot Battle** (University of Sydney, 2025) shows there is at least one live *sanctioned* bot-vs-bot Carcassonne competition ecosystem, with a public engine and a clean sandboxed bot protocol. Its ruleset is unusable for us (§1a), and the contestant bots are not published — but if a similar event recurs on standard rules it would be a legitimate, ToS-clean venue for an out-of-lineage rating, and the organisers are reachable. Filed as intel, not as a proposal.
