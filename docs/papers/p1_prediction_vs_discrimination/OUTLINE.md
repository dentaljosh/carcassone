## 0.1 Abstract — DRAFT

> Hand-crafted evaluation functions still beat learned value functions in some
> search-based game-playing systems, and the usual explanations — too little capacity,
> the wrong representation, the wrong training target, a weak teacher — are rarely
> separated experimentally. We report a pre-registered, multi-front negative result from
> a research program on 2-player Carcassonne (base game with farmers): across training
> targets (scalar, structured, clairvoyant, fair-information), a 25× model-capacity
> ladder (386K→10M parameters), a representation-independence probe (learners handed the
> heuristic's *own* features and exact-solver labels), and a tabula-rasa self-play
> arm, no learned value function beat the incumbent hand-crafted leaf evaluation at
> ranking sibling moves. The decisive experiment isolates the mechanism: a value head
> trained on the strongest corpus the program can produce (2,400 games / 345,333
> positions generated by the deployed champion at full search budget) *improved* at its
> own objective — held-out value↔outcome correlation r = 0.680, above both its parent
> (0.656) and the hand-crafted heuristic's reference (≈0.61) — while losing sibling
> move-discrimination to that same heuristic by 2.1× in mean regret (1.99 vs 0.95 points
> per decision), 9× at top-1 move agreement (0.067 vs 0.610), and 32× in Kendall tau
> (0.019 vs 0.615), judged against an exact endgame solver on 1,119 positions (paired
> sign-z = −17.4, against a pre-registered bar). The value refinement was statistically
> inert on discrimination even against its own parent (sign-z +0.97) while changing the
> selected move at 72% of positions: outcome regression optimizes a position-level
> signal that is nearly orthogonal to the between-sibling contrasts search consumes. The
> instrument is not blind — the same apparatus detects learned *policy-prior* gains
> (+35.7 ± 12.3 elo pooled, n=800 deck-paired) and a heuristic re-tune (+48.8 elo,
> z 3.1) — and the evaluation methodology (deck pairing, pre-registration, an exact
> solver as non-circular ground truth) is reported in full. We state the scope honestly:
> one game, one rule set, ≤10M-parameter networks, corpora of thousands (not millions)
> of games. Within that scope the result is closed on every axis we could afford to
> test, and it predicts where learned evaluation should and should not be expected to
> pay in other domains.

## 1. Introduction

**Content notes:**
- Open with the AlphaZero-era assumption: since Silver et al., the default recipe for
  game strength is search + a learned value function, and value-head quality (loss,
  outcome correlation) is routinely treated as a proxy for the search improvement it
  will buy. State the paper's thesis as the failure of that proxy: *outcome prediction
  and move discrimination are different quantities, and a value head can improve at the
  first while being ~30× worse than a hand-crafted incumbent at the second.*
- The pre-NNUE frame (advisor §2.3): classical engines with hand-tuned evaluations
  resisted learned replacements for years; our result is consistent with that era and
  gives it a measured mechanism at small scale.
- Contributions list (draft):
  1. A pre-registered closure ladder over the standard escape hatches (target /
     capacity / representation / tabula-rasa / teacher strength), each with its own
     falsifier, all returning the same verdict (§4).
  2. The mechanism experiment (CL-073): prediction improves, discrimination does not
     move — with a dissociation so clean the two heads agree on the chosen move at only
     27.7% of positions while scoring identically (§5–6).
  3. A non-circular measurement instrument (exact endgame solver as ground truth for
     sibling ranking) plus positive controls showing the instrument detects learned
     gains where they exist (§3).
  4. An honest account of what elo-style evaluation cannot answer here, including
     measured cross-band over-dispersion and depth-conditionality of "beats the
     heuristic" claims (§3.4, §4.6).
- Explicitly NOT claimed (front-load, per advisor): no claim about large scale, no
  claim about architecture families beyond those tested, no absolute-strength claim vs
  humans, no claim the game is canonical Carcassonne (Limitations §9).

## 2. Setting

**Content notes:**
- **The game:** 2-player Carcassonne, base tiles + farmers, no river/expansions.
  Imperfect information = the shuffled tile deck (identical public board). Why it is a
  good testbed: long horizon (~72 plies), deferred farm scoring, real hidden
  information, and an exactly solvable endgame region (K remaining tiles small).
- **The incumbent:** a hand-crafted "virtual score" leaf evaluation (v2.9-family,
  "curve125" meeple-commitment curve), ~a dozen additive terms over a union-find board
  decomposition (city/road/farm/cloister components, meeple economy, opponent
  anticipation), tuned over months by paired-game experiments. Cite the leaf-component
  knockout ablation (CL-074) in one paragraph as evidence the leaf's terms carry real,
  separable value (meeple-economy curve ≈ 300 elo of it).
- **The champion:** classical PIMC (perfect-information Monte Carlo over sampled deck
  determinizations) with PUCT, heuristic-leaf priors, pooled-Q argmax, k=8
  determinizations × 1,376 sims = 11,008 sims/move, exact K≤2 marginalized endgame
  handoff (CL-071 promotion). The champion is *net-free* — this matters for §5: the
  training corpus cannot be blamed on a weak neural teacher.
- **The learned track's history in one figure:** policy priors are the one learned
  component that ever paid (CL-067); learned *value* never did. This paper is the
  autopsy of the value half.

## 3. Instruments

**Content notes:**
- **3.1 The exact endgame solver.** Marginalized DP over the hidden deck for K
  remaining tiles ≤ 2 (K≤4 exists but ~21 min/solve); produces exact child values for
  every legal move at a root. Ground truth is *uncorrelated with the incumbent by
  construction* — the paper must reproduce the READOUT's §3.1 argument: the deep-search
  oracle alternative (h6400 action-Q) correlates 0.995 with the leaf, so scoring
  "learned vs leaf" on it is circular in the leaf's favour; the solver ruler is the
  harder, non-circular test, chosen before results existed.
- **3.2 The sibling-discrimination ruler.** 1,119 exact K≤2 marginalized roots (drawn
  from 10,067 deep-search sibling sets); metrics per ranker: mean argmax-regret in raw
  points (primary), top-1 agreement, Kendall tau-b; paired sign-z across rankers on
  identical solved roots (solve once, score many). Instrument integrity: the leaf arm
  reproduces regret 0.9508 / top-1 0.6095 / tau 0.6153 to 4 decimals across three
  independent runs months apart (see RESULTS_TABLES.md T1 note).
- **3.3 Game-play evaluation.** Deck-paired (same deck both seats) head-to-head, n=400
  paired standard, σ conventions (n=400 paired ≈ ±12 elo), pre-registration before
  first game, fresh-band confirms, selecting-cell exclusion. One paragraph, pointing at
  the P2 methodology corpus; this paper *uses* the discipline, P2 *is about* it.
- **3.4 Why elo alone cannot answer this question.** Three measured reasons:
  (a) depth-conditionality — the same learned champion reads +40.1 / +24.4 / −28.7 elo
  vs the same heuristic at 800/1600/3200 sims (one shared deck band, n=400/rung): a
  "beats the heuristic" elo claim reverses as the opponent deepens;
  (b) cross-band over-dispersion — identical configs read +0.9 / −53.4 / +20.9 on three
  deck bands (sd 2.20× nominal), harness exonerated by an identity control, so
  within-band deck-paired contrasts are the only defended class (and are what this
  paper's game results use);
  (c) sims-washout — policy gains at low sims vanish under deep search, so a
  high-budget gate is blind to component-level differences. Hence the offline solver
  ruler as the discrimination instrument, with game play as confirmation for positive
  controls.
- **3.5 Positive controls (the instrument is not a blind ruler).** Same apparatus
  detects: policy-prior distillation +42.8 (gate band) and +28.7 (disjoint confirm
  band), pooled +35.7 ± 12.3, n=800 deck-paired at equal sims (CL-067); heuristic leaf
  re-tune +48.8 elo fair, z 3.13 (CL-051); and offline, a learned sibling *comparator*
  that beats the leaf at held-out ordering (CL-034, regret −41%) — which then fails to
  convert through search, itself a datum for §6.

## 5. The headline result (CL-073)

**Content notes:**
- Design: warm-start from the best policy-distilled net (CL-067 iter_03), refine on the
  strongest corpus the program can produce — 2,400 games / 345,333 rows generated by
  the *net-free* champion at the full k8×1376 = 11,008 budget — with the value loss
  up-weighted 5× (plus lr 3e-4 cosine, 4 epochs, split BY GAME 2,280/120 files).
  Pre-registration committed before the ruler ran (commit `9660f67`); bar: strictly
  lower solver regret than the champion's own curve125 leaf AND paired sign-z ≥ +2.
- The training *worked on its own diagnostic*: held-out value↔outcome r 0.6564 →
  0.6795, above the ≈0.61 heuristic reference; train value MSE 0.100 → 0.046.
- The verdict: regret 1.9946 vs 0.9508 (2.10×), top-1 0.0670 vs 0.6095, tau 0.0190 vs
  0.6153 (±0.0053 vs ±0.0120 bootstrap), paired 91 better / 523 worse / 505 tie,
  sign-z −17.43, mean Δregret +1.0438 pts/root, Δτ −0.598 (z −44.6). NO by 8× the bar.
- The two sharper facts (READOUT §4.3 — these are the paper's centerpiece, not the
  verdict):
  (a) vs its own parent the refine is *inert on discrimination* (161/144/814, sign-z
  +0.97, Δτ +0.0013) — the value objective moved (+0.023 r), discrimination did not
  move at all;
  (b) the two heads pick a *different* child at 72.3% of roots while scoring
  identically badly — not two imperfect orderings, but noise around a common
  position-level signal.
- Caveats that travel with the numbers (from the READOUT, verbatim discipline): the
  +0.6795 is within-window and position-level; the 1,119 roots are all K=2 endgames;
  offline regret is not online search value; one recipe, one seed (but the 2026-06
  sweep record covers the objective/capacity/architecture/target axes).

## 6. Mechanism — why outcome regression does not buy move discrimination

**Content notes (the section the advisor says makes this a mechanism paper):**
- **What MSE-to-outcome optimizes:** position-level calibration against a label whose
  variance is dominated by everything *both* players do after the decision — the label
  is (almost) common to all siblings. Between-sibling contrasts are a small-signal
  residual on top of a large shared component; the head can reduce its loss massively
  by learning the shared component and never resolving the residual. **MEASURED
  2026-08-02 (gap G3 closed) — this is now figure F-G3, not a hand-wave:**
  [measurement/paper_g3_20260802/G3_VARIANCE_DECOMP.md](../../../measurement/paper_g3_20260802/G3_VARIANCE_DECOMP.md).
  The training label is *exactly* common within a game (max within-game range of |label|
  = 0.0 over 2,400 games / 345,333 rows; zero residual variance once game × side-to-move
  is known; no sibling sets in the corpus at all). Supplied counterfactually on the
  1,119-root exact-solver bank: 99.72% between-root / 0.28% between-sibling, so a
  root-mean-only predictor scores R² = 0.99719 with Kendall tau = 0 by construction.
  Figure data table ships as `G3_FIGURE_DATA.csv` (3 panels).
- **What argmax needs:** exactly the residual. Kendall tau against exact child values
  is the operational definition; the leaf's advantage is *globally calibrated absolute
  values* (CL-065's finding) — its additive terms were tuned by game outcomes of
  paired play, i.e., tuned *through* the discrimination channel.
- **Supporting measured facts:**
  - value ESS is games, not positions (CL-066 probe: memorization at ~1,200 labels);
    2,400 games is the data regime of every kill — sibling contrasts from outcome
    labels may only become learnable at AZ scale (this is the honest scale-out).
  - M2's heads: outcome-tracking correlation *rising* (0.50→0.65) while tau stays flat
    at 0.02 across 5 iterations — the dissociation is stable under training, not a
    one-off.
  - The LTR counterpoint: a *ranking-objective* learner over action-conditioned
    features CAN beat the leaf offline (CL-034: regret −41%, top-1 0.464→0.535) — so
    discrimination is learnable when the objective targets it — and that offline win
    then fails to convert through search (all 4 integrations at sims=200 lose to plain
    search-with-leaf; search itself collapses static decisive regret 6×). Two-sided
    lesson: the objective is the binding axis offline, and search is a second,
    independent filter online.
  - F13 side-story (one paragraph, resolved empirically): the suspected tanh-range
    defect ("residual targets ±2 into a ±1 head") is CONFIRMED as code and REFUTED as
    mechanism — 0 of 52,971 regenerated rows exceed the range (max |Δ| = 0.822); the
    real defect on that axis was loss-weight magnitude (~414× policy:value imbalance).
    Kills the cheap "it was just a bug" explanation.
- **The one-line mechanism statement (candidate pull-quote):** "The axis the value
  objective improves is not the axis search consumes."

## 7. Implications

**Content notes:**
- **When learned value should help:** when the evaluator's job is position-level
  (pruning, time management, resignation thresholds, win-probability display) — our
  head is the best outcome predictor the program produced, and those uses are open.
  When training signal exists at the *decision* level (comparison training, TD-leaf on
  move choices, NNUE's training on search-backed evaluations of positions from played
  games) — connect in Related Work.
- **When it cannot (at this scale):** replacing a decision-tuned incumbent at move
  ranking, funded by outcome labels alone, with games (not positions) as the effective
  sample unit.
- **What this predicts for other domains:** any system where (a) a hand-tuned
  evaluator was optimized end-to-end through play, (b) learned replacement is trained
  on outcome regression, and (c) data is game-limited — expect prediction gains that do
  not convert. Candidate examples for discussion (no new claims): pre-NNUE chess
  evaluation, imperfect-information PIMC settings. Note explicitly what NNUE did
  differently (trained on search values of real-play positions at scale — i.e., moved
  both the objective and the data regime).
- **Advice to practitioners (the transferable checklist):** measure sibling
  discrimination directly against a non-circular oracle before funding integration;
  treat value-loss/correlation improvements as non-evidence for search gains; keep a
  positive-control channel (policy priors here) so nulls are attributable to the
  component, not the ruler.

## 9. Limitations

**Content notes (each with its advisor-mandated phrasing):**
- **9.1 The walled variant (disclose proactively; "a limitations paragraph, not a
  wound — but only if disclosed proactively").** The vendored engine's start tile sits
  at row 6 of a 35×35 grid (6 rows headroom above vs 28 below), so rule-legal
  placements above row 0 were silently never offered: 67.8% of games had ≥1 denied
  placement, 21.7% of tile plies, 2.6% of all legal placements, 100% of denials on one
  side (commit `b7d61ab`, 400-game paired diagnosis; independently corroborated at
  70.8% by a shift probe). REQUIRED phrasing: every experiment in this paper was played
  on this *variant*; the defect is symmetric between players and identical across arms,
  so **all paired contrasts — the entire kill ladder and both positive controls —
  remain internally valid**; what is NOT claimed is any statement about canonical
  Carcassonne, and no absolute game-science statistic is reported here. (The border
  has since been root-caused and a recentring fix built behind flags in the Rust port;
  cite as remediation-in-progress, DECISIONS 2026-07-31/08-01 entries.)
- **9.2 No external human anchor — and why this paper does not need one.** Every claim
  is internal-comparative (learned vs hand-crafted under identical search, identical
  corpora, paired decks) or solver-grounded (exact child values). Absolute strength vs
  humans is neither claimed nor needed; say this plainly and cite the in-ecosystem-elo
  caveat as the reason the paper leans on the solver ruler. (Advisor: "P1 and P2 do not
  need the anchor.") The word "superhuman" does not appear.
- **9.3 Scale and architecture family.** Networks ≤10M params; corpora ≤3,600 games
  per arm; conv/MLP/GBDT/ridge families tested, transformers not. CL-064 answers
  capacity, not architecture family. Either (a) run the bounded modern-architecture
  control (a transformer-style ranker at the same data budget — advisor: ~1–2 box-days
  + a GPU day, worth ~1 referee objection) or (b) scope the claim "at this scale" in
  the abstract and here. **Decision for Joshua — see CLAIMS_LEDGER.md G2.**
- **9.4 Single domain.** One game, one rule set, one incumbent heuristic. The
  mechanism argument (§6) is general; the measurement is not.
- **9.5 Endgame-weighted discrimination ruler.** The 1,119 exact roots are all K=2;
  midgame discrimination is untested by the instrument (mitigation on record: the
  deployed system plays the exact solver in that region anyway, so a value head that
  merely matched the solver there would be redundant by construction; and CL-034/CL-037's
  offline probes on the 10,067-root deep-search pool span the whole game).
- **9.6 Rules-fidelity class.** The engine is a patched fork of an unmaintained
  open-source implementation; the project itself found and fixed a farm-scoring
  correctness bug mid-program (the farmer-adjacency involution fix, DECISIONS
  2026-05-29), and the border saga later surfaced additional latent edge-of-board
  defect classes (negative-index wrap, fatal last-row/column accesses — none reachable
  in the reported corpora; DECISIONS 2026-08-01, lockstep-fuzz amendment). Present the
  rule-conformance test suite + the Rust-port bit-exactness gates (G1–G6: full-record
  replay, 10⁴-game lockstep fuzz, 3.34M leaf values) as the trust argument (advisor
  §5.2). If `docs/RULES_FIDELITY_AUDIT_*.md` lands before submission, cite it here.
- **9.7 Multiplicity.** Dozens of screens ran across the program. Defenses to
  foreground: pre-registration before first game (commits on record per experiment),
  screen-vs-confirm separation, selecting-cell exclusion from pools, documented
  winner's-curse refutations, and a false-negative audit cutting the *other* way
  (~50% of underpowered kills were wrong — the program over-killed, not over-claimed).
